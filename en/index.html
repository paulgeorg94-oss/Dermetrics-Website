Moles and skin cancer

When should I see a dermatologist about a mole?

Come if a mole is new, changes or looks unusual. The ABCDE rule can help:

  • Asymmetry: one half looks different from the other.
  • Border: the edge is irregular or blurred.
  • Colour: the spot has several shades.
  • Diameter: larger than 6 mm.
  • Evolution: size, shape or colour change.

A mole that looks quite different from your other moles (the «ugly duckling») should also be checked. A single sign is enough for a medical assessment.

More: How can I spot skin cancer early myself?

What does skin cancer look like at the beginning?
  • Black skin cancer (melanoma): an irregular, often multicoloured spot that grows or changes. Some melanomas are raised and firm and grow within weeks (EFG signs: elevated, firm, growing).
  • White skin cancer (basal cell carcinoma): often a pearly, shiny nodule with small blood vessels. It grows slowly, sometimes bleeds or does not heal. It can also look like a flat, scaly, reddish patch.

Any skin change that bleeds, weeps or does not heal after a few weeks should be examined.

More: Basal Cell Carcinoma

How often should I have my moles checked?

Look at your skin yourself regularly (see the guide to skin self-examination). How often a full-body medical check makes sense depends on your risk, for example the number of moles, unusual moles, skin cancer in the family and your skin type. We set the interval together with you. After a previous skin cancer, regular checks are usually needed for life.

More: How can I spot skin cancer early myself?

What does a skin cancer check cost, and who pays for it?

A full-body skin check takes about 20 minutes and costs approx. CHF 160. Depending on the indication, the costs are covered by basic insurance or supplementary insurance. Whether and to what extent your insurance pays depends on your policy. Ask your insurer in advance.

Is a removed mole examined?

Yes. Every removed mole and every skin change suspected of cancer is examined under the microscope (histologically) in the laboratory. This makes it certain whether the change was benign or malignant.

How is basal cell carcinoma (white skin cancer) treated?

Treatment depends on the risk of recurrence, that is, on location, growth pattern and size.

  • Low risk: surgical removal with a safety margin; for superficial tumours also creams, freezing or photodynamic therapy.
  • Higher risk (for example on the face or after recurrence): micrographically controlled surgery (Mohs) or surgery with complete margin control. If surgery is not possible, radiotherapy is an option.
  • Advanced: discussion in the interdisciplinary team.

We clarify which approach suits you in the medical assessment.

More: Basal Cell Carcinoma · Mohs surgery · Photodynamic therapy (PDT)

Do I need follow-up checks after skin cancer surgery?

Yes. After any skin cancer treatment, regular checks are important to detect recurrences and new skin tumours early. How often is decided according to findings and risk. The checks are often useful for life.

Removal of skin lesions

How are moles, fibromas, seborrhoeic warts and lipomas removed?

The method depends on the lesion:

  • Moles: shave removal or complete removal with a narrow safety margin, depending on the suspicion.
  • Fibromas and skin tags: removal with scalpel, scissors or electrocautery.
  • Seborrhoeic warts (seborrhoeic keratoses): scraping, freezing or laser.
  • Lipomas (fatty lumps): minor surgery with a skin incision.

We discuss which method is suitable for you before the procedure.

More: Benign skin lesions

Does removal hurt? Will there be a scar?

We work under local anaesthesia. You feel a brief pulling sensation during the injection, and usually no pain during the procedure. Afterwards the area may be tender for a few days. A small scar, redness or pigment change is possible, especially with larger lesions or surgery with stitches. With superficial procedures, usually little remains visible.

How long does healing take?

Superficial procedures usually heal faster than surgery with stitches. With stitches, the threads are removed after a while, and the scar then still needs a long time to mature. How long it takes for you depends on the site, size and method. We discuss this before the procedure.

Scars

How are scars, keloids and acne scars treated?

Treatment depends on the type of scar.

  • Raised scars and keloids: silicone sheets or gels, cortisone injections into the scar, pressure dressings.
  • Other options: freezing from within, laser, combinations. Some procedures are off-label (e.g. botulinum toxin or 5-fluorouracil for keloids).
  • Acne scars: depending on the scar type, laser, microneedling, peels or targeted minor procedures.
  • Pronounced keloids: removal, combined with follow-up treatment to prevent recurrence.
How long does it take for a scar to fade?

Fresh scars are often red and slightly raised at first. Maturation takes a long time. Some raised scars still improve on their own over time. If a scar bothers you, itches or grows, an assessment is worthwhile.

What helps with raised or red scars?

Silicone products are used as the first measure, applied regularly and over a longer period. For more pronounced scars or itching, cortisone injections are added. Laser can additionally reduce redness and thickness.

Chronic skin conditions

Psoriasis: what helps, and is it contagious?

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammation of the skin and not contagious. According to current knowledge it cannot be cured, but it can be treated well.

  • Mild forms: creams and ointments (e.g. cortisone, vitamin D derivatives), light therapy.
  • Moderate to severe forms: tablets or injections that act specifically on the immune system, including biologics. These also work when the joints are affected.

We clarify which therapy fits according to extent, accompanying conditions and your wishes.

Hidradenitis suppurativa: what is it, and what helps with lumps in the armpits or groin?

Hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa) is a chronic inflammation of the hair follicles. It causes painful lumps and abscesses in skin folds such as the armpits and groin. Later, tunnels under the skin and scars can develop. It is not contagious and has nothing to do with poor hygiene.

Treatment depends on severity: ointments, antibiotics, biologics or minor surgery. Lifestyle (e.g. stopping smoking, weight) also plays a role.

More: Hidradenitis suppurativa

Atopic dermatitis and eczema: what helps with the itching?

The basis is consistent skin care with moisturising creams. During active flares, cortisone creams or anti-inflammatory ointments without cortisone help. Cooling and avoiding irritants such as wool can relieve the itching. For severe cases, there is light therapy or medication that acts throughout the body.

We treat atopic dermatitis and eczema in the practice.

Wounds

Why is my wound not healing?

When a wound does not heal, there is often an underlying cause that also needs treatment: circulatory disorders of the arteries or veins, diabetes, infections, persistent pressure or malnutrition. Without treating this cause, the wound often does not heal despite good wound care.

Have a wound that does not heal assessed by a doctor.

What should I do about a leg ulcer (ulcus cruris)?

For a leg ulcer caused by diseased veins, compression is central, that is, bandages or stockings. In addition there is moist wound care, cleaning of the wound, exercise and treatment of the diseased veins. Before compression, the arteries must be checked, because too much compression can cause harm if circulation is impaired. So do not start compression on your own.

When should I see a specialist about a wound?

Come if your wound does not improve over a longer period despite treatment, if there are signs of infection (redness, warmth, pus, fever), if a circulatory disorder is suspected or if the cause is unclear.

If you have a fever or rapidly increasing redness, seek medical help immediately.

Acne

What is acne, and where does it come from?

Acne is a common inflammation of the sebaceous glands. Several factors interact: a lot of sebum, blocked hair follicles, skin bacteria and inflammation. Hormones and hereditary predisposition influence it. Acne has nothing to do with poor cleanliness.

More: Acne · Acne and rosacea

What types of acne are there?

There are mild forms with blackheads and small spots, and more severe forms with inflamed nodules and deep cysts. The type and severity determine the treatment. Rosacea and hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa) must be distinguished from acne. These are separate conditions with different treatment.

More: Acne · Rosacea · Hidradenitis suppurativa

When should I see a dermatologist about acne?

Come if over-the-counter care does not help after a reasonable time, if the acne is inflamed, deep or painful, if it leaves scars or if it is a psychological burden for you. The earlier treatment begins, the more likely scars can be avoided.

What helps against acne?

Treatment depends on severity.

  • Mild acne: creams and gels that keep the pores clear and reduce bacteria (e.g. retinoids, benzoyl peroxide).
  • Moderate acne: additionally oral antibiotics or, for women, in some cases hormonal treatment.
  • Severe or scarring acne: an oral medication that strongly reduces sebum production (isotretinoin). It requires medical supervision and check-ups.

Good skin care and patience are always part of it.

How long does it take for treatment to work?

Acne treatments do not work overnight. Patience and consistent use are needed. At first the skin may even become drier or more irritated. We discuss with you what to expect and when we adjust the treatment.

What is isotretinoin, and what do I need to bear in mind?

Isotretinoin is a strong medication for severe or persistent acne. It can have side effects, such as dry skin and dry lips, and it requires medical check-ups. It must not be used during pregnancy, because it can seriously harm the unborn child. Women of childbearing age therefore need reliable contraception. We clarify in the consultation whether it is an option for you.

May I squeeze spots?

Better not. Squeezing can push the inflammation deeper, and scars and dark marks can develop. Have persistent spots treated instead.

Do chocolate, fat or poor hygiene cause acne?

Poor cleanliness does not cause acne. Washing too often and too vigorously can even irritate the skin. The evidence on diet is inconsistent. A clear effect of particular foods has not been shown for everyone affected.

Which skin care and make-up are suitable for acne?

Use mild cleansing and light care products that do not block the pores (look for «non-comedogenic»). Sun protection is important, especially if you use acne treatments that make the skin more sensitive. We discuss with you which products fit your treatment.

Adult acne: why do I still have spots at 30 or 40?

Acne is not just a teenage issue. Adults, especially women, also have acne, often on the lower face and jaw. Hormonal fluctuations, predisposition and stress can play a role. Adult acne can also be treated well.

What helps with acne scars and dark marks after acne?

Depending on the type of scar, laser, microneedling, peels or targeted minor procedures can help. Dark marks after acne (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation) often fade over time and can also be treated. It is important to bring the active acne under control first.

More: Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation · Mosaic 3D

What is the difference between acne and rosacea?

Both cause redness and spots. Rosacea usually affects the central face, tends to occur in adulthood, comes with redness and visible small blood vessels and has no blackheads. The treatment is different. A medical assessment brings clarity.

More: Rosacea

What is the difference between acne and hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa)?

Despite the similar name, they are different conditions. Acne mainly affects the face, chest and back. Hidradenitis suppurativa affects skin folds such as the armpits and groin, with painful lumps and abscesses. The treatment differs.

More: Hidradenitis suppurativa
