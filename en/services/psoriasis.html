Psoriasis

Treatment by a dermatologist in Zurich

A chronic inflammation that affects more than the skin. Modern therapies inhibit the inflammation in a targeted way.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich Enge (district 2, Selnau), we treat psoriasis. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 15 November 2026

In short: Psoriasis is a chronic inflammation that occurs in flare-ups. It can also affect the joints and is more often accompanied by other conditions. For moderate to severe psoriasis, there are biologics that inhibit inflammatory messengers in a targeted way. Whether starting treatment early permanently changes the course of the disease is being researched and has not yet been conclusively proven.

Overview

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory disease that occurs in flare-ups. Typical are red, scaly patches of skin, often also on the scalp, fingernails and toenails. Misdirected immune cells and inflammatory messengers play a central role, above all interleukin-17 and interleukin-23. Around 170,000 people in Switzerland live with psoriasis.

Psoriasis is not contagious and has nothing to do with poor hygiene. The predisposition is inherited. Flare-ups are triggered or worsened, for example, by infections, medication or stress.

The inflammation is not limited to the skin. Up to 3 out of 10 people affected develop joint inflammation (psoriatic arthritis) over time. Cardiovascular disease, diabetes, overweight, chronic inflammatory bowel disease and psychological strain occur more often than in the rest of the population. The World Health Organization therefore classifies psoriasis as a serious chronic disease.

According to studies, people who develop the disease before the age of 40 more often have a course that progresses faster from mild to severe. Depending on severity, treatment applied to the skin and light therapy, also combined with a vitamin A acid derivative, are the first choice (first-line therapy).

Treatment options

Treatment applied to the skin

A cream with calcipotriol and betamethasone, a combination of a vitamin D derivative and cortisone. For long-term control of the skin, calcineurin inhibitors may be considered. In psoriasis this is use outside the approved indication (off-label), which we discuss with you beforehand.

Light therapy, also combined

Light therapy can be combined with a vitamin A acid derivative in tablet form, for example acitretin. For light therapy, we work with a specialised partner practice.

Conventional systemic therapy

Medicines that act not only on the skin but throughout the body, as tablets or injections. They are considered for more severe psoriasis or joint involvement and are often the step before a biologic.

Biologics

Biotechnologically produced antibodies specifically inhibit individual inflammatory messengers, above all interleukin-17 or interleukin-23. In studies, a high proportion of those treated achieve completely or almost clear skin. They require careful medical monitoring.

Treating early and consistently: what research says

In dermatology, the «hit hard and early» approach is being studied: treating early and consistently. It is based on the observation that skin with long-standing psoriasis develops an «inflammatory memory». Specialised immune cells and changes in gene regulation cause the inflammation to flare up again and again in the same places.

Research is examining whether early use of biologics, usually within the first 1 to 2 years after onset, can prevent this memory. In a study of more than 850 adults, the skin was more often completely clear after 20 to 28 weeks when the disease duration was short (about 44 out of 100 compared with 28 out of 100). In an observational study, people who started systemic therapy early less often developed severe psoriasis after 10 years.

Important limitation: The approach has not yet been conclusively proven by long-term data. Older studies found no connection between disease duration and response. In addition, there are not yet any reliable markers that predict who will remain symptom-free without medication in the long term after early treatment.

Important with acitretin

Acitretin belongs to the retinoids, which are related to vitamin A. It can seriously harm an unborn child. Women must not become pregnant during treatment and for up to 3 years afterwards, and must use reliable contraception without interruption from one month before the start, during treatment and for 3 years afterwards. A pregnancy test is carried out before treatment begins.

Blood must not be donated during treatment and for 3 years afterwards, because the blood could harm an unborn child. Acitretin often raises blood lipid levels. We discuss these points with you in detail before treatment.

Procedure

  • Examination of the skin, questions about joint complaints and accompanying conditions
  • Assessment of severity and quality of life
  • Discussion of treatment options and their side effects
  • For biologics: tests before starting therapy and application for cost approval
  • Regular check-ups, as the disease is chronic

Possible side effects and limitations

  • Biologics are highly effective medicines. Rarely, infections among other things can occur.
  • Every treatment has its own side effects, which are discussed before starting.
  • Psoriasis usually needs long-term treatment.

Costs and health insurance

In Switzerland, biologics are generally covered by basic insurance (OKP), minus deductible and co-payment, if the conditions of the FOPH list of pharmaceutical specialities (limitatio) are met. They are prescribed by specialists in dermatology. Cost approval from the health insurer is required before starting therapy. The limitatio usually requires moderate to severe psoriasis and often previous light or systemic therapy. Earlier use must be justified in the individual case; approval is not guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

What is psoriasis?

Psoriasis is a chronic inflammatory disease that occurs in flare-ups. Misdirected immune cells and inflammatory messengers play a central role.

Is psoriasis contagious?

No. Psoriasis is not contagious and has nothing to do with poor hygiene.

Can psoriasis affect the joints?

Yes. Up to 3 out of 10 people affected develop joint inflammation over time, known as psoriatic arthritis. That is why we ask about joint complaints at every examination.

Which treatment comes first for psoriasis?

Depending on severity, creams, for example with calcipotriol and betamethasone, and light therapy, also combined with a vitamin A acid derivative such as acitretin, are the first choice. For moderate to severe psoriasis, systemic therapies and biologics are added.

What are biologics for psoriasis?

Biologics are biotechnologically produced antibodies. They specifically inhibit individual inflammatory messengers, above all interleukin-17 or interleukin-23, and are used for moderate to severe psoriasis.

Can psoriasis be cured?

Psoriasis is a chronic disease. Research is investigating whether starting therapy early allows long-lasting freedom from symptoms in some of those affected. This has not yet been conclusively proven.

Why see a dermatologist early for psoriasis?

Because accompanying conditions and joint involvement should be recognised early, and studies give indications that starting therapy early can have a favourable effect on the course.

Does health insurance pay for biologics for psoriasis?

Yes, if the conditions of the FOPH list of pharmaceutical specialities are met and the health insurer has granted cost approval in advance. Deductible and co-payment apply.
