Acne and rosacea

Treatment of inflamed facial skin

Both conditions often affect the face but are treated differently.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich district 2 (Selnau/Enge), we treat acne and rosacea. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 3 November 2026

In short: Acne and rosacea are inflammatory skin conditions that often appear on the face but are treated differently. Depending on the form and severity, creams, tablets or, for severe acne, isotretinoin may be considered. Which treatment suits you and how long it lasts is determined individually by the doctor.

Overview

In acne, the sebaceous glands are inflamed. Blackheads, pimples and, in severe forms, nodules develop. Rosacea, by contrast, shows mainly as redness of the face, visible fine blood vessels and sometimes pustules.

Treatment depends on the form. For acne, it ranges from creams to tablets. For severe acne with nodules, the European guideline recommends isotretinoin. Antibiotic tablets should generally not be taken for longer than three months. In women, hormonal treatment may be considered in addition.

For rosacea, recognising and avoiding triggers as well as creams are part of the treatment. For pronounced redness, there are additional creams that reduce the redness temporarily. For more severe forms, tablets may be considered. If the eyes are involved, an additional assessment by an ophthalmologist makes sense. You can find more about rosacea in our patient information on rosacea.

Options

Acne: treatment on the skin

Various active ingredients in a cream or gel that act on the pores and the inflammation.

Acne: tablets

For more pronounced acne, antibiotics for a limited time and, in women, hormonal treatments may be considered.

Acne: isotretinoin

A tablet treatment for severe acne. It is prescribed and monitored by the doctor. Special precautions, for example regarding pregnancy, are discussed.

Rosacea: creams and tablets

For rosacea, creams are used and, for more severe forms, tablets as well. Triggers such as heat or certain irritants are discussed.

Procedure

  • Examination of the skin and classification of form and severity
  • Discussion of the treatment options and your goals
  • Start of treatment, with information about side effects for tablets
  • Check-ups to assess effect and tolerability

Duration and side effects

  • Acne and rosacea treatments take time. An effect often only shows after several weeks.
  • Creams can irritate or dry the skin at the beginning.
  • With tablets, check-ups and information are important. This applies especially to isotretinoin.
  • How long a treatment lasts is determined by the doctor in each individual case.

Costs and health insurance

Whether an examination or treatment is medically justified (indication) is decided by the doctor in each individual case. If it is medically justified, it can be billed to compulsory basic health insurance. If there is no medical indication, the treatment is provided as a self-pay service. We discuss the costs with you before the treatment.

Frequently asked questions

How do acne and rosacea differ?

Acne shows as blackheads, pimples and nodules. Rosacea shows mainly as redness, fine visible blood vessels and sometimes pustules on the face. A medical examination clarifies which condition is present.

How is acne treated?

Depending on severity, with creams, tablets or, for severe acne, isotretinoin. The doctor makes the choice after the examination.

When is isotretinoin considered for acne?

The European guideline names isotretinoin for severe acne with nodules. It is prescribed and monitored by the doctor.

How long does acne need to be treated?

That varies. An effect often only shows after several weeks. The duration is determined in each individual case.

What helps with rosacea?

Treatment includes creams and avoiding triggers. For pronounced redness and more severe forms, there are further options. Which one is suitable is discussed after the examination.

Does health insurance pay for the treatment of acne and rosacea?

If there is a medical indication, the treatment is billed to basic insurance. Purely cosmetic treatments are provided as a self-pay service.

A treatment works best when it matches the form of the condition.
