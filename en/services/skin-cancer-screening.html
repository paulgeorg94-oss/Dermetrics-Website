Skin cancer screening

Full-body skin check in Zurich

The skin is examined systematically for conspicuous changes. How often this makes sense depends on your personal risk.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich district 2 (Selnau/Enge), we offer skin cancer screening. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 3 November 2026

In short: During skin cancer screening, the doctor examines your entire skin for conspicuous spots and moles, usually with a dermatoscope (a skin surface microscope). How often check-ups make sense depends on your personal risk and is determined by the doctor. A 3D full-body scan is a possible addition if it is medically justified.

Overview

During skin cancer screening, the whole skin is examined, including the scalp, back, feet and the spaces in between. Conspicuous areas are assessed with a dermatoscope. This is a skin surface microscope that allows a view into the upper layers of the skin.

People at increased risk include, for example, those with a very large number of moles, fair skin, previous skin cancer, relatives with melanoma or a weakened immune system. Whether and how often a check-up makes sense for you is determined by the doctor in each individual case.

The science is cautious here: whether regular examinations reduce mortality in people without a particular risk has not been reliably proven. Medical societies therefore require that the benefits and possible disadvantages are discussed beforehand. One of the disadvantages is that harmless changes may also be removed.

Options

Full-body skin check

The entire skin is examined. Conspicuous areas are assessed more closely with the dermatoscope.

Skin self-examination

Medical societies recommend keeping a careful eye on your own skin. We show you what to look out for.

3D full-body scan (DeviScan)

A photographic documentation of the entire skin that allows changes to be compared over time. See the section below.

Investigation of conspicuous areas

If an area remains unclear, it can be removed and examined histologically in the laboratory.

Procedure

  • Conversation about your skin, previous skin changes and possible risk factors
  • Examination of the entire skin, with a dermatoscope for conspicuous areas
  • Discussion of the findings in plain words
  • If necessary: small removal under local anaesthesia and tissue examination
  • Joint decision on when the next check-up makes sense

The 3D full-body scan (DeviScan)

In a 3D full-body scan, the skin is recorded photographically and the images are compared at later appointments. The scan, too, must be medically justified (indication). Without a medical indication, it is a self-pay service.

What is known today: So far, there is no proven advantage of this system over the normal skin cancer check-up by the doctor. In a study of people at high risk of melanoma, additional 3D photography led to more removals of skin areas without more melanomas being found.

Our medical experience: In everyday practice, the scan can be helpful. It makes changes traceable and measurable, which makes the assessment more objective (objectification of diagnostics). This is experience from practice, not evidence from studies.

Disadvantages and limits

  • The examination itself is painless.
  • Changes may be removed that later turn out to be harmless.
  • A removal leaves a scar.
  • An examination cannot rule out that new changes develop later.

Costs and health insurance

The costs of skin cancer screening are covered by basic insurance or supplementary insurance, depending on the indication. Whether an examination is medically justified (indication) is decided by the doctor in each individual case. We discuss the costs with you before the examination.

Frequently asked questions

From what age should you have skin cancer screening?

There is no single point in time that applies to everyone. What matters is your personal risk. After a conversation and an examination, the doctor determines whether and when check-ups make sense.

How often does a skin cancer check-up make sense?

That depends on the risk. With an increased risk, closer check-ups are usually agreed; with a low risk, less often or as needed. The interval is determined by the doctor in each individual case.

Does health insurance pay for skin cancer screening in Switzerland?

The costs of skin cancer screening are covered by basic insurance or supplementary insurance, depending on the indication.

How does skin cancer screening work?

After a short conversation, the entire skin is examined. Conspicuous areas are examined more closely with the dermatoscope. We then discuss the findings and the next steps. 3D scans are taken at the Zurich-Altstetten location, and you receive the results by email within 72 hours. The online Pre-Check is a statistical orientation tool, not a diagnosis.

Is a 3D full-body scan better than the normal skin check?

An advantage over the normal skin cancer check-up has not been proven so far. In our experience, the scan can make diagnostics more traceable, especially for follow-up checks. It must be medically justified.

What happens if a mole is conspicuous?

The area is assessed more closely. If it remains unclear, it can be removed under local anaesthesia and examined in the laboratory. We discuss the result with you.

A skin check makes sense when it matches your personal risk.
