Benign skin lesions

Removal for aesthetic or medical reasons

Many skin lesions are harmless. They are removed if they bother you or if they need to be investigated.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich district 2 (Selnau/Enge), we treat benign skin lesions. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 3 November 2026

In short: Benign skin lesions such as seborrhoeic keratoses, warts or fibromas can be removed if they bother you or if they need to be investigated. The doctor assesses the lesion beforehand. If there is no medical indication, the removal is provided as a self-pay service. Which procedure is suitable depends on the type and location.

Overview

Benign skin lesions include, for example, seborrhoeic keratoses, skin tags (fibromas), common warts, moles and yellowish deposits on the eyelids. They are usually harmless.

Before any removal, the lesion is assessed by the doctor. Some lesions can resemble skin cancer. If an area remains unclear, it is examined histologically in the laboratory.

For common warts, salicylic acid and freezing are about equally effective in studies. Freezing has somewhat more side effects, especially when it is applied vigorously. For other lesions, depending on the findings, options include freezing, ablation, laser or a minor operation.

Options

Medical assessment

First, it is clarified what kind of lesion it is and whether removal is necessary or advisable.

Freezing

The lesion is treated with cold. This is used, for example, for warts.

Ablation or laser

Superficial lesions can be ablated or treated with a laser.

Minor operation

Some lesions are cut out and, if necessary, examined in the laboratory.

Procedure

  • Assessment of the lesion, with the dermatoscope if necessary
  • Discussion of the procedures and the costs
  • Removal, usually under local anaesthesia
  • Tissue examination if the lesion is unclear
  • Wound care as instructed

Scars and side effects

  • After a removal, a scar or a change in pigmentation may remain. The risk depends on the procedure, the body site and the skin.
  • Redness, crusts and mild pain are possible temporarily.
  • Some lesions can come back after removal, for example warts.

Costs and health insurance

Whether an examination or treatment is medically justified (indication) is decided by the doctor in each individual case. If it is medically justified, it can be billed to compulsory basic health insurance. If there is no medical indication, the treatment is provided as a self-pay service. We discuss the costs with you before the treatment.

Frequently asked questions

Which skin lesions can be removed?

For example seborrhoeic keratoses, warts, fibromas, moles and xanthelasmas. Whether removal makes sense is discussed after the assessment.

Does a scar remain after removal?

That is possible. The risk depends on the procedure, the body site and your skin. We discuss it before the procedure.

Does a removed skin lesion have to be examined?

If a lesion is unclear, it is examined histologically in the laboratory. This provides certainty that nothing is overlooked.

How are warts treated?

Often with salicylic acid or freezing. Both are about equally effective in studies; freezing has somewhat more side effects.

How long does the removal take?

Small lesions can often be removed in a short time. The exact duration depends on the type, number and location of the lesions.

Does health insurance pay for the removal?

If there is a medical indication, for example suspected skin cancer or symptoms, the treatment is billed to basic insurance. Purely aesthetic removals are provided as a self-pay service.
