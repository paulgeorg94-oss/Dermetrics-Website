What is a treatment with artificial daylight (Healite II)?

PDT stands for "photodynamic therapy". A special cream (Metvix) is applied to the skin. This cream makes altered skin cells (for example actinic keratoses, i.e. "light damage" of the skin) sensitive to light. The Healite II lamp then uses artificial light modelled on daylight to destroy these cells in a targeted way. Healthy skin is hardly affected.

Important: No real sunlight is used. The treatment does not depend on the weather, because it is carried out indoors in the practice with the Healite II lamp.

What is the treatment used for?

The treatment is used mainly for actinic keratoses. These are rough, scaly patches of skin that develop from many years of sun exposure and are considered a precursor of skin cancer. They usually affect the face and scalp.

What do scientific studies show about this treatment?

In a larger study at several dermatology practices in Germany using artificial daylight lamps such as the Healite II lamp, most patients showed a clear reduction of the treated skin lesions after three months. Almost all patients (more than 9 out of 10) had no or only mild pain during the treatment. The large majority of patients and practitioners were satisfied with the result (source 1).

How does the treatment work?

  • 1. Sun protection: First, a special sunscreen is applied to all areas of skin that will later be treated. It protects against UV light.
  • 2. Skin preparation: After a short time, the skin is gently prepared. Scales and crusts are carefully removed so that the cream can be absorbed better.
  • 3. Applying the cream: The Metvix cream is applied thinly to the affected areas of skin.
  • 4. Contact time: The cream is left on for 1 hour.
  • 5. Light treatment: The illumination with the LED light of the Healite II lamp then begins. This light resembles natural daylight and acts gently on the skin. The illumination takes 10 to 30 minutes, depending on the severity of the actinic damage and the depth and intensity of the therapy needed.
  • 6. After the lamp: The cream is washed off.

What should I bear in mind after the treatment?

  • The treated skin may be red, swollen or slightly scaly for a few days. This is a normal reaction and subsides on its own.
  • On the day of treatment, the skin should be protected from strong sunlight – for example with clothing, a hat or by staying at home for the rest of the day.
  • No make-up or other creams should be applied to the treated skin until it is no longer irritated (if in doubt, ask the practice).
  • If the skin is very painful, changes unusually or shows signs of infection (e.g. pus, severe redness, fever), please contact the practice.

Important notes

  • The treatment should not be carried out during pregnancy, with certain skin conditions or with a known hypersensitivity to the cream – this is clarified in the practice beforehand.
  • Regular skin checks by a dermatologist are recommended, even after successful treatment, as actinic keratoses can recur.

Frequently asked questions

Does the treatment hurt?

Most patients feel no or only very mild pain during the treatment. A slight tingling, burning or feeling of warmth is possible; this is normal.

How often does the treatment need to be repeated?

Usually 1 to 3 sessions are needed, depending on the extent of the skin lesions. The success of the treatment is usually checked after about three months.

Does the treatment depend on the weather?

No. The treatment does not depend on the weather, because it is carried out indoors in the practice with the Healite II lamp.
