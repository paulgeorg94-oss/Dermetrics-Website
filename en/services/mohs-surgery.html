Mohs surgery

Micrographically controlled surgery

A step-by-step procedure in which the surgical margins are examined systematically so that as little healthy tissue as possible is lost.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich district 2 (Selnau/Enge), we treat skin tumours with Mohs surgery. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 3 November 2026

In short: Mohs surgery, also called micrographically controlled surgery, removes a skin tumour step by step. After each step, the margins are checked under the microscope. This shows whether tumour tissue is still present. Whether the procedure is suitable for you is decided by the doctor in each individual case.

Overview

In Mohs surgery, the tumour is removed in thin layers. Each layer is checked microscopically at the edges and at the base. If tumour is still found, further tissue is removed specifically at that spot. In this way, as precisely as possible only as much tissue as necessary is removed.

The procedure is considered above all for skin tumours with a higher risk of recurrence or where healthy tissue should be particularly spared, for example on the face. Guidelines provide for a choice between this procedure and conventional surgery depending on the risk of recurrence.

The evidence on an advantage over normal surgery for first-time basal cell carcinomas is limited. The difference in recurrences is not clearly established in the medical literature. Surgical procedures overall have lower recurrence rates than, for example, cream treatments.

Options

Step-by-step removal

The tumour is removed in layers until the margins are free of tumour.

Microscopic margin control

The margins are examined completely, not just as samples.

Wound closure

Once the tumour has been completely removed, the wound is closed. For larger defects, we work with plastic surgery at Clinic Selnau.

Procedure

  • Discussion of whether the procedure is suitable for you
  • Removal of a first layer under local anaesthesia
  • Microscopic examination of the margins
  • If necessary: a further layer at the affected spot
  • Wound closure as soon as the margins are free of tumour

Advantages and limits

  • Advantage: the margins are checked systematically and healthy tissue is spared.
  • Limit: the procedure can involve several steps. The duration depends on the individual case.
  • Limit: not every tumour is suitable for the procedure.
  • As with other operations, side effects such as bleeding after surgery, wound infection or impaired wound healing are possible.

Costs and health insurance

Whether an examination or treatment is medically justified (indication) is decided by the doctor in each individual case. If it is medically justified, it can be billed to compulsory basic health insurance. If there is no medical indication, the treatment is provided as a self-pay service. We discuss the costs with you before the treatment.

Frequently asked questions

What is Mohs surgery?

It is a surgical procedure in which a skin tumour is removed layer by layer. After each step, the surgical margins are checked under the microscope.

Who is Mohs surgery suitable for?

It is considered for certain skin tumours, for example with a higher risk of recurrence or at sites where tissue should be spared. The doctor makes the decision after the examination.

How long does a Mohs operation take?

Because the procedure takes place in steps, the duration depends on how many steps are needed. This can only be roughly estimated in advance.

Is Mohs surgery better than a normal operation?

For first-time basal cell carcinomas, an advantage has not been clearly established in studies. The procedure has the advantage of systematic margin control. Which approach is suitable is decided in each individual case.

Is Mohs surgery painful?

The procedure is carried out under local anaesthesia. After the procedure, wound pain may occur, which can usually be treated with common painkillers. We discuss this with you.

Who pays for Mohs surgery?

If there is a medical indication, for example proven skin cancer, the treatment is billed to basic insurance. If there is no medical indication, it is provided as a self-pay service.
