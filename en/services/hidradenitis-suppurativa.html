Hidradenitis suppurativa

Acne inversa

A chronic inflammation of the skin in the body folds. Medication and surgery can be combined.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology, at Clinic Selnau in Zurich district 2 (Selnau/Enge), we treat hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa). All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Updated: 3 November 2026

In short: Hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa) is a chronic inflammation of the skin in the body folds with painful nodules, abscesses and tunnels under the skin. It is not contagious. Depending on severity, it is treated with medication, biologics or surgery. Which treatment is suitable for you is determined by the doctor.

Overview

Acne inversa, medically called hidradenitis suppurativa, mainly affects body folds such as the armpits, groin and the skin under the breasts. Painful nodules, abscesses and tunnels under the skin develop. The condition usually begins in young adulthood.

The condition is not contagious and has nothing to do with poor hygiene. It runs a chronic course, often with flare-ups.

Depending on severity, treatments on the skin, antibiotics, biologics and surgery may be considered. For moderate to severe disease, guidelines also provide for combining a biologic with surgery. Surgery ranges from opening individual tunnels (deroofing) to larger removal of diseased tissue.

Options

Treatment on the skin and with antibiotics

For milder forms, local treatments and antibiotics may be considered.

Biologics

Biologics are used for moderate to severe disease. Approval and cost coverage are clarified in advance.

Deroofing

The roof of a tunnel under the skin is removed. With suitable findings, this is possible under local anaesthesia.

Surgical removal

Individual nodules or larger diseased areas can be removed surgically. The approach depends on the findings.

Procedure

  • Examination of the affected body regions and assessment of severity
  • Discussion of the options: medication, biologics, surgery or a combination
  • Treatment and wound care
  • Regular check-ups, as the condition is chronic

Possible side effects and limits

  • After surgery, symptoms can come back, including at other sites.
  • As with any procedure, bleeding after surgery, infection and impaired wound healing are possible.
  • Medications have side effects, which are discussed before starting.
  • The condition usually needs long-term treatment.

Costs and health insurance

Whether an examination or treatment is medically justified (indication) is decided by the doctor in each individual case. If it is medically justified, it can be billed to compulsory basic health insurance. If there is no medical indication, the treatment is provided as a self-pay service. We discuss the costs with you before the treatment.

Frequently asked questions

What is hidradenitis suppurativa (acne inversa)?

It is a chronic inflammation of the skin in the body folds. Painful nodules, abscesses and tunnels under the skin are typical.

Is hidradenitis suppurativa contagious?

No. The condition is not contagious and has nothing to do with poor hygiene.

What helps with hidradenitis suppurativa?

Depending on severity, treatments on the skin, antibiotics, biologics and surgery may be considered. Several methods are often combined.

When does surgery make sense for hidradenitis suppurativa?

Guidelines provide for surgery, among other things, for individual nodules, tunnels and permanent tissue damage. Whether and which procedure is suitable for you is determined by the doctor on the basis of the findings.

What is deroofing?

The roof of a tunnel under the skin is removed so that the area can heal. With suitable findings, this is possible under local anaesthesia.

Does health insurance pay for the treatment of hidradenitis suppurativa?

If there is a medical indication, the treatment is billed to basic insurance. Special conditions apply to biologics, which are clarified in advance.

With a chronic condition, what counts is a plan that holds up over a longer period.
