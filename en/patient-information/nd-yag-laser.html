Nd:YAG laser

Wavelength 1064 nm

A laser system established in dermatology that works deep in the skin and usually largely spares the surface.

At the Dermetrics practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg at Clinic Selnau, Zurich Selnau/Enge, we discuss with you whether this treatment is an option for you. View all services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Last reviewed: 11 November 2026

In short: The Nd:YAG laser heats the skin in a targeted way in deeper layers, while the surface usually remains largely intact. It is used, for example, for small blood vessels, redness, pigment spots and skin rejuvenation. Results develop over weeks to months. It can soften signs of skin ageing, but cannot reverse them. Sun protection remains the most important prevention.

Overview

The neodymium-doped yttrium aluminium garnet laser, Nd:YAG laser for short, has been used in dermatology for years. This page refers to the 1064 nm wavelength, which is the one most commonly used for skin rejuvenation. The laser is one of the non-ablative or minimally ablative lasers: the top layer of the skin (epidermis) usually remains largely intact, and the effect takes place in deeper layers of the skin.

How it works: The laser light is absorbed by certain target structures in the skin, mainly water, but also melanin (skin pigment) and haemoglobin (the red blood pigment). This produces controlled, limited heating. It can close fine blood vessels, influence excess pigment, damage hair follicles during hair removal and provide a targeted stimulus in the middle layer of the skin (dermis).

Depending on the pulse duration, a distinction is made between long-pulse, Q-switched and picosecond Nd:YAG lasers. They deliver the energy differently and are therefore used for different goals: blood vessels, pigment, hair removal or skin rejuvenation.

Effect on skin ageing and skin rejuvenation

An important effect is based on so-called dermal remodelling: the controlled heat in the dermis activates the body's own repair processes. According to the specialist text on which this page is based, this can contribute to the following:

  • increased formation of new collagen and elastic fibres
  • greater skin thickness and firmness
  • finer lines and wrinkles and a better skin structure
  • a more even complexion with less redness, fewer dilated small blood vessels and fewer pigment spots
  • possibly finer pores and a better skin texture

How pronounced this will be for you cannot be predicted.

Important: The effects are not visible immediately. New collagen formation takes time, and the result develops over weeks to months. In studies, the result was usually only fully developed after three to six months. Several sessions are usually needed. The laser can soften existing signs of skin ageing caused by sun damage or natural ageing. However, it does not reverse them and does not permanently prevent further skin ageing.

Other areas of use

In addition to skin rejuvenation, the Nd:YAG laser is used, among other things, for:

Small blood vessels and redness

Spider veins, superficial small blood vessels and redness, also in rosacea.

Pigment

Age spots and certain pigmentation disorders.

Hair removal

The laser damages the hair follicles.

Acne and acne scars

For active acne and acne scars.

Benign skin lesions

Certain benign skin changes.

At Dermetrics, we offer the full range of these applications.

Procedure, side effects and aftercare

Treatment procedure

  • Before treatment, the skin is usually cleansed; in some cases cooling or an anaesthetic cream is applied.
  • During treatment, a brief stinging or warming sensation is possible.
  • Depending on the type of laser and the treatment goal, several sessions are usual. Usually 2 to 4 sessions are needed, two to four weeks apart.

After treatment: what is normal?

  • Temporary redness and slight swelling, usually for hours to a few days.
  • Occasionally slight crusting with more intensive settings.
  • A high sun protection factor is particularly important in the weeks afterwards.

Possible side effects

The specialist text describes the treatment as having comparatively few side effects, also in darker skin types, as the laser absorbs only little melanin. Rarely, the following can occur:

  • temporary hyperpigmentation (darker spots)
  • redness or swelling lasting several days
  • in individual cases blisters or scars, for example with incorrect settings

When should you get in touch? In the case of persistent redness, pain, blisters, signs of infection or unusual skin changes after treatment. Please contact the practice in that case.
