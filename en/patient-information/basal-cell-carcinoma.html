Basal Cell Carcinoma

Non-melanoma skin cancer

The most common form of skin cancer: development, surgical treatment and follow-up care.

At Dermetrics, the practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg at Clinic Selnau in Zurich Selnau/Enge, we treat basal cell carcinoma. All services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Last reviewed: 25 September 2026

Overview

Basal cell carcinoma is the most common form of skin cancer. Unlike squamous cell carcinoma, it correlates less directly with cumulative sun exposure over a lifetime and more strongly with intense sun exposure, particularly in childhood, as well as with genetic factors: the underlying cause is usually alterations in the so-called Hedgehog signalling pathway, often in the PTCH1 gene.

Basal cell carcinoma typically grows slowly and locally destructively, and only very rarely metastasises, but if left untreated it can grow deeply into surrounding tissue. It typically appears as a pearly, shiny nodule with fine blood vessels visible on its surface, often with a raised border or a central depression.

Treatment Options

Surgical excision with complete margin control

Basal cell carcinoma is primarily treated surgically. Every tumour undergoes complete, margin-controlled histological work-up by our laboratory, Kempf und Pfaltz. This ensures a very reliable, complete surgical excision that is well above average.

Two-stage approach in the T-zone

In the T-zone — the eyebrows, forehead, nose and eye area — excision and wound closure are always carried out in two separate procedures: first the tumour is completely removed, and only once it has been fully excised and no tumour cells remain is the wound closed, in a second procedure. This is the only way to safely carry out the subsequent complex reconstruction, for example using flap surgery.

After treatment of a basal cell carcinoma, consistent follow-up care is essential: a regular full-body skin cancer screening at intervals determined individually according to risk, optionally complemented by a full-face resurfacing and rejuvenation programme, along with the proactive prevention programmes of the Dermetrics concept, to help prevent new lesions at an early stage.
