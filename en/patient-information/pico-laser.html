Pico laser

Picosecond laser

A laser system with extremely short light pulses, used mainly for pigment, scars and tattoos.

At the Dermetrics practice of Dr. med. Paul J. Georg at Clinic Selnau, Zurich Selnau/Enge, we discuss with you whether this treatment is an option for you. View all services

Responsible: Dr. med. Paul J. Georg, specialist in Dermatology and Venereology · Last reviewed: 11 November 2026

In short: The pico laser emits extremely short light pulses in the picosecond range. They act mainly mechanically rather than through heat and break pigment, tattoo ink and melanin down into small particles that the body clears. It is used for pigment spots, melasma, scars, skin rejuvenation and tattoos. The result depends on the skin findings and the skin type.

Overview

The pico laser emits light pulses in the picosecond range; a picosecond is one trillionth of a second. This ultra-short pulse duration mainly produces a mechanical, so-called photoacoustic effect in the tissue. With older types of laser, for example Q-switched lasers, heat is the main factor instead.

How it works: Colour pigments, tattoo ink and melanin (skin pigment) are broken down into very small particles. The body's own immune system then clears these particles. According to the specialist text on which this page is based, the surrounding skin is placed under less strain than with older types of laser.

What is the pico laser used for?

The pico laser was originally developed for tattoo removal. Its areas of use in dermatology have since expanded.

Tattoos

The laser breaks down the tattoo ink, which the body then clears. According to the specialist text, it is still considered the gold standard for this: tattoos of a wide variety of colours can often be removed in fewer sessions than with older laser systems.

Pigment spots

Age spots (lentigines), sun spots, freckles, café-au-lait spots and naevus of Ota. According to the specialist text, they respond well to treatment, also in darker skin types.

Melasma

A pigmentation disorder that is difficult to treat and mainly affects women. The pico laser can be used as a complement, often combined with topical active ingredients such as tranexamic acid, which according to the specialist text can further improve the results. We assess the use of such active ingredients medically.

Acne scars

For mild to moderate sunken (atrophic) scars, the fractional pico laser can stimulate collagen formation and improve the skin structure, usually with shorter downtime than other types of laser.

Skin rejuvenation

Fine lines, enlarged pores, uneven skin structure and general light damage to the skin (photoageing).

Stretch marks and scars

For pale stretch marks (striae albae), the improvements that can be achieved are usually moderate. Surgical scars or scars caused by injury can be treated as a complement to other therapies.

How pronounced the result will be for you cannot be predicted. At Dermetrics, we offer the full range of these applications.

Procedure, side effects and aftercare

Treatment procedure

  • Depending on the area of use, several sessions are needed, usually 2 to 4, 2 to 8 weeks apart.
  • A local anaesthetic cream can be applied beforehand.
  • During treatment, a brief stinging sensation is possible, which some people compare to the snap of a rubber band.
  • Duration of a session: usually only a few minutes to about half an hour.

After treatment: what is normal?

  • Temporary redness, slight swelling or pinpoint bleeding (petechiae) in the treated area. These usually subside within a few days.
  • With pigment spots and tattoos, the treated area may briefly appear whitish and then form a crust.
  • According to the specialist text, downtime is generally low, and you are usually only slightly restricted in everyday life. Downtime: Low, usually only slight redness, for 3 to 5 days at most.

Possible side effects

  • Temporary darkening of the skin (post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation). The risk is somewhat higher in darker skin types.
  • Consistent sun protection before and after treatment is important to reduce this risk.

How often and how strongly side effects occur depends on the skin findings, skin type and area of use. We discuss this with you before treatment.

Who should not receive the treatment?

  • Wound-healing disorders
  • active tumours
  • active skin infections or open wounds in the treatment area
  • pregnancy (as a precaution, even though no particular risks are known)
  • taking certain medicines that make the skin more sensitive to light
  • freshly tanned skin, because of the risk of pigment changes
  • a tendency to keloids (raised, thickened scars): particular caution is needed with certain scar treatments

Before treatment we clarify in a medical consultation whether it is suitable for you. We discuss previous illnesses, medicines and your skin type. The number of sessions, the result and possible side effects depend strongly on the skin findings, skin type and area of use.

When to get in touch

If anything is unusual

In the case of persistent redness, pain, blisters, signs of infection or unusual skin changes after treatment. Please contact the practice in that case.
